# DarkFactory Design Context

## Authority

The complete web design source of truth is [`design-system/darkfactory/MASTER.md`](design-system/darkfactory/MASTER.md). Read it before designing or implementing any public or authenticated interface.

A file under `design-system/darkfactory/pages/` may contain page-specific deviations only. It inherits every Master rule that it does not explicitly override. Do not duplicate the Master into page files or invent a second design system.

## Users

DarkFactory serves developers and AI agents who need a domain-neutral, production-grade starter they can understand and adapt. The interface must help them:

1. Learn the architecture and boundaries.
2. Sign in with seeded development accounts.
3. Exercise a complete vertical slice from UI through Postgres and observability.
4. Inspect realistic forms, data, permissions, and application states.
5. Turn the starter into a product without first removing a fictional business domain.

The starter is opinionated about engineering and developer experience, but intentionally unopinionated about industry, customer, or business model.

## Brand Personality

**Engineered, candid, refined, modular, quiet confidence.**

- Explain architecture plainly; do not hide demo or optional behavior.
- Prefer precise, restrained copy over hype.
- Let type, spacing, rules, and hierarchy provide character.
- Make every section and component feel composable.
- Avoid visual or verbal claims that the starter cannot prove.

## Aesthetic Direction

The unifying aesthetic is **engineered editorial**: warm tinted neutrals, strong sans-serif type, purposeful asymmetry, crisp boundaries, restrained depth, and sparse functional color.

### Public surface

Use [Squarespace](https://www.squarespace.com/) as inspiration for editorial restraint, generous whitespace, strong type, asymmetric composition, and deliberate media placement. Do not copy its branding or layouts. Public pages should form a useful neutral family—such as Home, Architecture, Vertical Slice, Components, Documentation, About, and Sign in—rather than a repeated generic landing template.

Generic page content may use `placehold.co`, fake avatars, and fake favicons. Label demo evidence honestly, reserve media dimensions, and never present fake metrics, logos, activity, or testimonials as real proof.

### Authenticated portal

Use [shadcn blocks](https://ui.shadcn.com/blocks) as the continual composition reference for shells, sidebars, authentication, account settings, forms, tables, and empty states. Preserve DarkFactory tokens and voice rather than copying block styling verbatim.

The portal must prioritize task clarity: a clear responsive shell, scannable hierarchy, robust forms and collections, complete empty/loading/error states, and discoverable theme controls. It must demonstrate architecture without pretending to be a vertical product.

## Typography

All typography is sans serif.

- **Headings and display:** Manrope, weights 500–700.
- **Body and interface:** Public Sans, weights 400–700.
- Do not use Libre Bodoni or any serif.
- Do not use Inter, Roboto, Arial, or Open Sans.
- Use the scale, line heights, tracking, and line-length limits defined in the Master.

## Theme Direction

- Support light, dark, and system mode; never design dark-only.
- Support the ten Master palettes: Neutral, Slate, Blue, Cyan, Green, Amber, Orange, Red, Rose, and Violet.
- Warm neutral surfaces remain the visual foundation; palette color communicates action, selection, data, or state.
- Cyan and violet may be user-selected accents but must never become a purple/cyan gradient or glow aesthetic.
- Use semantic theme tokens only—no page-specific hex colors or arbitrary spacing.

## Design Principles

1. **Make the architecture legible.** Navigation, copy, and state should teach how the starter works.
2. **Stay domain neutral.** Use Feature Items, accounts, architecture, and capabilities—not invented sales, finance, health, or commerce concepts.
3. **Compose rather than decorate.** Favor typography, whitespace, alignment, and rules over card grids, icon tiles, gradients, or effects.
4. **Design every state.** Loading, empty, filtered-empty, error, disabled, success, unauthorized, and interrupted states are part of the feature.
5. **Keep interactions stable.** State changes must not alter layout bounds or create jump, bounce, scale, or hover translation.
6. **Use one system across two modes of work.** Public pages may be spacious and editorial; the portal may be denser and operational, but both share the same type, tokens, voice, and restraint.
7. **Accessibility is structural.** Semantic markup, predictable focus, contrast, and responsive reading order come before visual novelty.

## Non-Negotiable Interaction and Accessibility Rules

- Meet WCAG AA: at least 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text and meaningful UI graphics.
- All interactive targets are at least 44×44px.
- Provide visible, consistent `:focus-visible` rings and complete keyboard operation.
- Use meaningful icons from one coherent outline family; do not use emoji as structural icons.
- Do not communicate state with color alone.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` in CSS. Do not add a reduced-motion toggle, profile preference, database field, cookie, or local-storage setting.
- Prevent layout shift by reserving dimensions for images, skeletons, labels, counters, and async controls.
- Verify public and portal behavior at 375px, 768px, 1024px, and 1440px.
- Keep fixed navigation from covering content or focused elements; do not introduce page-level horizontal scroll.

## Explicit Anti-References

Reject dark-only developer-tool clichés; purple/cyan gradients; glow; glassmorphism; cyberpunk styling; gradient text; pure black or white surfaces; centered generic heroes; repetitive icon-card grids; nested cards; decorative dashboard metrics; large rounded icon tiles; serif type; vague AI hype; and invented business content.

When a design choice is not covered here, follow the Master. If the Master is silent, choose the quietest accessible option that reinforces the audience, use cases, and engineered-editorial direction.
